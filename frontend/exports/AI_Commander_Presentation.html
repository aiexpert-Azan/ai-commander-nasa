Mission design is hard to learn from a textbook

Students read about space missions. Engineers have to decide. That gap is where beginners lose interest.

Every choice is a trade-off

A bigger telescope means more mass, more power and more cost. Beginners read this, but rarely feel it.

Systems depend on each other

Pick a distant orbit and you also need a stronger rocket and a deep-space ground network.

Real science data feels out of reach

Exoplanet hunting means reading faint brightness dips that look like noise to a newcomer.

The AI Scientist · experimental
Model
1D convolutional neural network (TensorFlow), 166,849 parameters
Data
1,000 Kepler DR25 KOIs (500 candidates / 500 false positives), split by star 700 / 150 / 150
Held-out test (150 stars)
Accuracy 71.33% · candidate recall 94.67% · threshold 0.40
  • Runs live in the backend; no mock scores
  • A raw experimental score, never a calibrated probability
  • Flags many false positives as candidates; it can be wrong
  • Never confirms a planet: the player makes the call

Only the 150 test-split stars are playable, so these metrics are not an independent new benchmark.
